Based on the data, young Americans aged 15–29 have experienced a dramatic shift toward isolation, with their time spent alone increasing by 45% in 2023 compared to a 2010 baseline. This upward trajectory actually began well before the COVID-19 pandemic, as this youngest demographic was already spending 18% more time alone by 2019. While the arrival of the pandemic in 2020 caused a sharp, historic spike in alone time across all generations, the 15–29 age group has consistently maintained a significantly higher relative increase than any older bracket. By comparison, older age groups experienced much milder shifts, with the 60+ demographic showing the least relative change over the entire thirteen-year period.
